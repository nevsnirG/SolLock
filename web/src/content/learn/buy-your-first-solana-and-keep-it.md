Buying Solana takes an afternoon. Most of that afternoon is not the buying; it is setting things up so you still have the Solana in five years. This guide walks through both, in order, without assuming you have done any of it before.

It is about how, not whether. Solana's price moves a lot in both directions, and nothing here is a recommendation to buy. Start smaller than feels interesting.

## 1. Choose an exchange

An exchange is where you pay in regular money and receive crypto. Pick one that is licensed in your country, that sells Solana, and that lets you withdraw to your own wallet, which not every app does. SolLock [lists two](/affiliates) it has referral links with; it is a short list, not a ranking, and any licensed exchange works.

## 2. Verify your identity

Licensed exchanges have to know who their customers are, so expect to upload an ID and take a selfie before you can buy. This is normal and it is not a crypto thing: banks do it too. Services that skip it are usually ones to skip.

## 3. Buy a small amount first

Deposit money, buy a little Solana, and get used to the screens. Exchanges show the price per coin and let you buy a fraction; you do not need a whole one. The exchange now holds the Solana for you, the way a bank holds a balance.

## 4. Set up your own wallet

A wallet is an app that holds your own keys, so the Solana is yours rather than a balance at a company. Phantom, Solflare and Backpack are widely used on phones and in browsers; Solana's [wallet directory](https://solana.com/wallets) has more. When you create a wallet, it shows a recovery phrase of twelve or twenty-four words. Write it on paper, store it in two safe places, and never type it into any website or app that asks for it. No real service needs it, SolLock included.

Your wallet has an address, a long string of letters and digits. That is what you give to anyone sending you Solana, and it is safe to share.

## 5. Move the Solana to your wallet, in two steps

On the exchange, choose withdraw, pick Solana, and paste your wallet's address. Send a small amount first, wait for it to show up in the wallet, and only then send the rest. Transfers on Solana usually confirm within seconds and cost a fraction of a cent, so the test costs nothing worth mentioning and catches a mistyped address before it matters. The exchange may charge its own withdrawal fee.

Keep a little Solana in the wallet uncommitted: every action on the network needs a tiny [fee](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees), paid in Solana.

## 6. Decide what you are doing with it

Now the Solana is yours, and so are the decisions. Three common paths:

- **Hold it.** Most of the work is already done; [How to hold Solana for the long term](/learn/hold-solana-long-term) covers the rest of the setup.
- **Stake it.** Staking earns rewards in Solana for helping secure the network, with its own conditions. Your wallet can do it; [Locking vs staking Solana](/learn/solana-locking-vs-staking) explains what it is.
- **Lock part of it.** If you already know you want to hold for years and do not trust a future version of yourself in a crash, Solana can hold it for you until a date, enforced by the network. [Pick your date](/lock), or try a [free test lock](/lock?test) first with 0.01 SOL.

## Mistakes that cost people their first Solana

- Typing the recovery phrase into a website that "needs to verify" the wallet. It is a theft, every time.
- Sending to an address copied from a chat or an email rather than from the wallet itself.
- Leaving everything on the exchange for years because moving it felt hard.
- Buying more than they could leave alone, then selling it at the first fall.

The last one is the common one. [How to stop yourself from selling crypto in a dip](/learn/stop-selling-crypto-in-a-dip) is about exactly that. And if a word in this guide was new, [What is Solana, in plain words](/learn/what-is-solana) starts one step earlier.
