"Buy and hold" sounds like doing nothing. In practice the holding is the part people fail at, and when they fail it is rarely because the plan was wrong. They lose the recovery phrase, leave the coins on an exchange that disappears, or sell in the one week they promised themselves they would not. Holding for years is a setup, not a mood. Here is one that lasts.

This is a guide to the mechanics. It does not say whether to hold Solana at all. Its price can fall for years as well as rise, and a long hold is a decision to accept that.

## 1. Keep it in your own wallet

Coins on an exchange are a claim on that exchange. When FTX collapsed in November 2022, its customers found out what that means. For a long hold, move the Solana to a wallet whose recovery phrase only you have: Phantom, Solflare and Backpack are common choices, and a hardware wallet such as a Ledger keeps the keys off your computer altogether. Solana's own [wallet directory](https://solana.com/wallets) lists the options.

## 2. Back up the recovery phrase as if it has to last a decade

Because it does. Write the twelve or twenty-four words on paper or stamp them into metal, store two copies in two places, and never type them into a website or keep them as a photo. Test the backup once by restoring the wallet from it on a second device. Everything else in this guide depends on this step. A lock with a lost phrase is a lock on coins nobody will ever reach.

## 3. Decide the rules while the market is boring

Write down, in one paragraph, what would make you sell and what would not. A price fall is not a reason if the plan was to hold through falls. Reading it back during a crash is worth more than any chart, because it was written by the calm version of you.

## 4. Lock the part you have really decided on

Rules help until the day they do not. For the share you are sure about, Solana offers something firmer: a native account with a built-in lockup, which the network refuses to open before a date. Lock that share until a date with a reason, five years, ten, a birthday, and keep the rest where you can reach it. Before the date nobody can move the locked part, including you, so lock only what you could do without in a bad year. [How to lock Solana until a date you pick](/learn/how-to-lock-solana) walks through it, and a [free test lock](/lock?test) shows the whole thing with 0.01 SOL first.

## 5. Make the price harder to see

Delete the price widget, mute the group chat, keep the exchange app off your phone. People who check daily make more decisions, and more decisions is the opposite of holding. Pick a review date, once or twice a year, and look then.

## 6. Decide about staking on purpose

Staked Solana earns rewards in Solana, paid by the network for helping secure it, and it carries its own conditions: a few days to unstake, and the chance that a validator misbehaves. A SolLock lock is not staked and earns nothing. [Locking vs staking Solana](/learn/solana-locking-vs-staking) sets the two side by side so you choose rather than drift.

## 7. Keep records, and make sure someone else can find them

Note what you bought, when and for how much, for the tax rules where you live. And write down, for someone you trust, where the recovery phrase is and what it opens. A hold that only you know about ends with you.

## What this setup does not fix

It does not protect the price. Solana can be worth less in ten years than today, and holding does not change that. It does not remove the risk of a lost phrase, a phishing site or a wrong address; it only shrinks the number of moments in which those can happen. And a lock, once made, is a withdrawal ban with no exception for emergencies: [What can go wrong with a Solana time lock](/learn/solana-time-lock-risks) lists what to weigh first.

The account type behind the lock is in Solana's [stake account reference](https://solana.com/docs/references/staking/stake-accounts). The network enforces the date, so the setup holds without SolLock, and without you. For most people the hardest step is still number 3.
