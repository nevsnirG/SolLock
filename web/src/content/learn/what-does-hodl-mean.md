HODL is crypto's word for not selling. It began as a typing mistake, turned into a joke, and ended up as the name of a way to behave, with a small vocabulary of its own. This guide explains the words and says plainly what holding does and does not do.

## Where HODL comes from

On 18 December 2013 the price of Bitcoin was falling fast. On the Bitcointalk forum a user called GameKyuubi posted a message under the title "I AM HODLING". He meant "holding". In the post he explained, by his own account after some whisky, that he was a bad trader who would only sell low and buy back high, so he was not going to sell at all.

The misspelling stuck. Later people started reading it as an abbreviation, "hold on for dear life", but that came afterwards.

The original post is still the best definition. Holding is what you do once you admit that you cannot time the market.

## Diamond hands, paper hands and the rest

- **Diamond hands.** Holding through a big fall, or a big rise, without selling. The phrase came over from stock-trading forums in 2021.
- **Paper hands.** Selling at the first sign of trouble.
- **Buying the dip.** Buying more after the price has fallen.
- **FUD.** Fear, uncertainty and doubt: bad news, or whatever a holder would rather not hear.
- **Bag holder.** Someone still holding a coin that fell and never came back.

The last one belongs on the list as much as the first.

## What holding does, and what it does not

Holding takes away one risk: your own timing. Someone who does not sell cannot sell in a panic at the bottom, or buy back higher a week later.

It takes away no other risk. If a coin falls and stays down, the holder falls with it. Many coins that people once held with conviction are worth nothing today, and diamond hands on one of those meant losing all of it. Nobody knows what any coin, Solana included, is worth next year or in ten years.

So HODL is a way of behaving, not a promise. It suits money you have already decided you can leave alone, whatever happens to it.

## Why it is harder than it sounds

The sell button is one tap away, at every hour of every day. Prices fall fastest when the news is worst, and that is when everyone around you is selling. A plan made in a calm month feels like someone else's plan in a bad week.

[How to stop yourself from selling crypto in a dip](/learn/stop-selling-crypto-in-a-dip) is about that moment, and about why willpower is the wrong tool for it.

## What holders do to keep it up

- **Decide the rules first.** What would make you sell, and what would not, written down while the market is quiet.
- **Hold it yourself.** In a wallet whose recovery phrase only you have, backed up on paper. [How to hold Solana for the long term](/learn/hold-solana-long-term) has the whole setup.
- **Look less.** A review date twice a year beats a price widget.
- **Take the sell button away.** For the part you are sure about, a time lock does what diamond hands only claim to do: before the date nobody can move the locked Solana, you included. [How to lock Solana until a date you pick](/learn/how-to-lock-solana) explains how, and a [free test lock](/lock?test) shows it with 0.01 SOL for about a day.

A lock is final until its date, in an emergency too, and it does nothing about the price. Lock only what you could do without for the whole time.

The lock is Solana's own lockup, a field in an account that the network checks on every withdrawal. Solana's [stake account reference](https://solana.com/docs/references/staking/stake-accounts) describes it.
